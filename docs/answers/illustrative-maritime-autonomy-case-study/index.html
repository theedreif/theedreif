Maritime Autonomy Readiness: An Illustrative Scenario Case Study

A transparent hypothetical case study for testing human judgment when simulated uncrewed maritime-system data becomes stale.

By Ed Reif · 2026-11-16 · An educational framework; not a claim of independently validated results.

Evidence status: ILLUSTRATIVE — no field trial claimed

This is a constructed learning design example. It does not describe a real incident, a measured participant cohort, a specific company's system performance, client sponsorship or an operational deployment. No technical thresholds are invented.

The operating problem

A human operator supervising an uncrewed maritime exercise needs to notice that reported position data is no longer current enough to support a planned action. The educational objective is judgment under incomplete information.

Scenario canvas

Mission: maintain situational awareness during a supervised simulation. Signal: a position timestamp stops updating. Friction: the second team's message is delayed. Limit: verified exercise authority, platform procedures and stop rules govern the response. Decision: verify, hold or escalate under the approved exercise rules. Consequence: a branch produces further information or imposes a safe stop.

What would assessors look for?

  • Recognition of stale information, rather than blind trust in the display.
  • Clear separation of observed facts, assumptions and unknowns.
  • Decisions and escalation consistent with the provided operating limits.
  • A communication handoff confirmed by the receiving role.
  • An after-action explanation that accounts for uncertainty and alternative choices.

Evidence that would be needed

If this exercise were conducted, time-stamped observations, an authorised rubric, decision records, communications and a debrief might support a simulated-readiness assessment. Those records have not been collected for this example.

Update and retest

Change the message delay or data availability in a second scenario and compare evidence. Diagnose whether problems arise from detection, comprehension, constraints, interface design or communication. Where standards are unknown, consult authoritative technical and safety sources before any real exercise.
