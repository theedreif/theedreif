How to Design an Operational Readiness Scenario in 8 Steps

A practical step-by-step method using mission, signal, friction, limit, decision, consequence and evidence.

By Ed Reif · 2026-11-16 · An educational framework; not a claim of independently validated results.

Start with the person who must notice, decide and act—not the slide deck. This method adapts Ed Reif's Scenario Design Canvas.

Step 1 — Specify the mission

Name one outcome the performer must achieve. Describe the task and the real consequence of getting it wrong.

Step 2 — Define conditions and standards

Record roles, equipment, context, time, authority and the measurable performance standard. Unknown technical thresholds remain “requires authoritative verification.” Never invent them.

Step 3 — Select the critical signal

Choose a detectable change that should affect the person's plan: an anomaly, missing data or a revised priority.

Step 4 — Add realistic friction

Introduce one or two credible constraints. The aim is to reveal adaptation, not to trap the learner with arbitrary difficulty.

Step 5 — Bound the decision

State the possible choices, decision authority, safety limits, escalation and stop conditions. Make clear where judgment is required.

Step 6 — Design consequences

For each defensible action, prepare a plausible branch: continue, verify, pause, hand over, escalate or stop.

Step 7 — Capture observation

Use a rubric to record what was noticed, when, what decision followed, how communication worked and whether recovery was appropriate. A completion timestamp alone is insufficient.

Step 8 — Debrief and retest

Separate process quality from outcome luck. Identify gaps in knowledge, interface, support or procedure. Revise the scenario and test again.

Miniature worked example

Mission: supervise an uncrewed maritime-system exercise. Signal: position data becomes stale. Friction: a delayed status message. Decision: verify or escalate within prescribed exercise limits. Evidence: recognition, handoff and justified action. This is an illustrative scenario, not a claim about a deployed platform.
