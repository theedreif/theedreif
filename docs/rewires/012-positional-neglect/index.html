Why Position Changes Hand Value in Poker

Positional Neglect

Position is not a footnote. Acting later gives you more information before you spend chips and more control over how large the pot becomes.

By Ed Reif · The Poker Lobotomy

LEAKEvaluating starting hands without accounting for where you will act after the flop.
RULEThe same cards change value when your seat changes.
BETTER DECISIONTighten when you will act early, widen selectively when you have position, and price the informational advantage into every marginal decision.

Why this matters

Poker is a game of incomplete information, and position reduces that uncertainty. Acting after an opponent lets you see checks, bets, and sizes before deciding. That extra signal helps you realize equity, bluff more selectively, and avoid expensive guesses.

This is why a hand that looks playable on the Button may become a fold from early position. The cards did not change; the decision environment did.

Fast example

A suited broadway hand may have room to raise on the Button because you will often act last postflop. The same hand from early position can face several players behind, a 3-bet, and multiple streets of acting first. Position changes the cost of being wrong.
