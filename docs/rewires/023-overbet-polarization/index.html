Poker Overbets: Polarize or Don’t Use Them

The Overbet: Polarize or Don’t

An overbet tells a big story. Your range should usually support that story with hands that want maximum value and bluffs that benefit from maximum pressure.

By Ed Reif · The Poker Lobotomy

LEAKUsing overbets with medium-strength hands that do not want a huge pot and cannot comfortably face a raise.
RULEIf the range is not polarized, do not force a polarized size.
BETTER DECISIONOverbet when nut advantage, blockers, and opponent range support very strong value plus credible bluffs; use conventional sizes for merged medium-strength value.

Why this matters

Oversized bets change the opponent’s pot odds and push their continuing range toward stronger hands. That is powerful when you hold the top of range or a bluff chosen for good blocker properties. It is uncomfortable when you hold a hand that only wants calls from worse.

The size must fit the range construction. A large bet is not automatically “more pressure.” If it folds out the hands you beat and gets action mainly from hands that beat you, the extra pressure is being applied to yourself.

Fast example

With a medium-strength top pair, an overbet may chase away worse pairs and isolate you against stronger continues. With a nut-heavy range on a favorable river, the same size can extract maximum value while giving selected bluffs real leverage.
