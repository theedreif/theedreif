Slow-Playing Your Big Hands Costs More Than You Think

Slow-Playing Your Big Hands Costs More Than You Think

Heads-up sizing does not automatically transfer to multi-way pots. With more players in the hand, somebody is more likely to have connected strongly.

By Ed Reif · The Poker Lobotomy

LEAKUsing large heads-up continuation-bet sizes against several opponents.
RULEMore opponents usually mean less fold equity and a stronger continuing range.
BETTER DECISIONUse smaller, more selective bets when the multi-way structure supports betting, and reserve large pots for stronger range and hand advantages.

Why this matters

A bet in a multi-way pot has to travel through several ranges. Each player adds possible pairs, draws, two-pair combinations, and sets. That makes automatic large continuation bets expensive when your range is not clearly advantaged.

Smaller sizing can still deny equity, collect value, and clarify ranges while reducing the cost of running into resistance. The key is not “always bet small”; it is “do not pretend three opponents behave like one.”

Fast example

On a board that mildly favors your range, a one-third or even smaller pot bet can pressure weak holdings across several players without committing the amount a large heads-up c-bet would risk.
